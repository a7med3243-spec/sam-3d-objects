# Cloud Photo Frame – print & assembly guide

Final approved design (v2, lightweight): outer **324 × 234 × 16.5 mm**, 58 cloud bubbles (Ø20 mm),
photo **290 × 200 mm** (no glass), sliding back panel with a fold-out stand.
Finished weight ≈ **235 g** + photo.

## Files (`print_files/`)

| Plate | File | Size (mm) | Filament* |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `cloud_frame_1_front_frame.stl` / `.3mf` | 324 × 234 × 16.5 | ≈ 100 g (85 g frame + 15 g break-away helpers) |
| 2 | `cloud_frame_2_back_panel_with_stand.stl` / `.3mf` | 324 × 230 × 8 | ≈ 150 g |

\*Layer-by-layer estimate with the settings below; Bambu Studio will show the exact value.
Total ≈ 250 g PLA to print, ≈ 235 g finished.

The back panel is hollowed out on the photo side (closed 0.8 mm back skin + 1 mm ribs about 30 mm
apart). From the outside it looks the same as a solid panel.

STL and 3MF contain the same geometry. Use either.

## Printer settings (Bambu Lab A2L, Bambu Studio)

- **Material:** PLA (matte white looks closest to the preview).
- **Plate:** textured PEI gives a nice matte back. Use a little glue stick if the frame lifts.
- **Orientation:** keep the files as they are, with the flat back face down. Don't flip them.
  Keep the **long side along X (left–right)**. 324 mm only fits along the 330 mm axis.
- **Layer height:** 0.16 mm (0.12 mm for extra-smooth bubbles).
- **Walls:** 2. **Top/bottom:** 5 / 4 layers.
- **Infill:** front frame **8 % gyroid**, back panel **10 % gyroid**. Use per-object settings,
  or 8–10 % for both. More infill only adds weight, because the ribs are already solid.
- **Supports: OFF.** The front frame already contains its own break-away helpers (see below).
- **Brim:** none (there is no room on the bed for plate 1 anyway).
- Leave *elephant-foot compensation* at the default (0.15 mm).

## After printing

**Plate 1 – front frame.** Two thin helper pieces print with it, and both are loose parts:
1. **Lip ring**: a thin rectangular wall inside the photo opening. Lift it out.
2. **Comb**: thin fins under the top border (the slot where the back panel enters). Pull it out of the top edge.

**Plate 2 – back panel.** The leg is printed *already attached* (print-in-place hinge).
Grab the leg's foot (the two toes sticking out at the bottom) and swing it out and back
a few times until it moves freely. It stops by itself at the stand angle.

## Using the frame

1. **Photo:** 290 × 200 mm, normal photo paper (up to ~0.4 mm). The window shows
   284 × 194 mm, so 3 mm of each edge hides under the lip. The panel behind the photo is a rib grid,
   so if your photo paper is very thin or curled, put a sheet of thin card behind it.
2. **Close:** lay the frame face-down on a soft cloth and place the photo face-down in the
   opening. Slide the back panel in **from the top edge** along the side rails and push until it **clicks**.
3. **Open:** frame face-down, push up at the two **thumb dimples** near the top until the
   panel clicks free, then slide it out.
4. **Desk:** lift the leg at the small notch next to its foot and swing it out. It locks at
   **72°** (frame leans back 18°), with a stand base of ≈ 112 mm.

## Hanging on the wall

The two key-holes are in the **front frame** (left and right side, near the top). The frame
hangs directly on the screws, and the back panel just rests inside it: gravity keeps it closed.

- **Screws:** pan or round head, **head Ø 6–7 mm**, shank ≤ 3.5 mm (e.g. M3.5 / #6),
  head thickness ≤ 3 mm.
- **Spacing:** **305 mm** apart, level with each other.
- **Height:** the screw ends up ≈ **27–28 mm below the top edge** of the frame.
- Leave about **2.5 mm** between the wall and the underside of the screw head
  (the frame's back skin is 2 mm and the head pocket behind it is 4 mm deep).
- Push the heads into the round holes, then let the frame drop ≈ 3 mm so the heads lock behind the 2 mm back skin.
- Fold the leg flat before hanging.

## What was checked in the model

- Both files are clean, closed (watertight) solids and fit the 330 × 320 mm bed.
- Back panel and folded leg slide the full 230 mm without touching the frame. Only the two
  detent bumps meet their ridges (the "click") in the last 2–6 mm.
- The leg swings freely up to its stop and then hits the stop (stand angle 72.0°).
- Standing: the centre of mass sits ≈ 33 mm behind the front contact, while the foot is
  112 mm behind it (stable).
- Key-holes have ≥ 1 mm of plastic around them everywhere, and ≥ 1 mm to the side rail.
- Print-in-place gaps: hinge 0.35 mm, sliding parts ≥ 0.15 mm, break-away helpers 0.2 mm.
- Light-weighting: the rib grid stays clear of the rails, click detents, stand channel and
  hinge. All of the checks above were re-run on the lightweight panel.

These were checked on the 3D model only. The parts to watch on a first print are the hinge
(it should move freely after a few swings) and the click (if it is too stiff, sand the two
small bumps on the panel's side tongues).

## Changing the design

Everything is generated by `cloud_frame.py` (Python + `manifold3d`, `trimesh`):

```bash
pip install manifold3d trimesh numpy networkx
python3 cloud_frame.py S        # final design -> build/S/*.stl + report.json
```

The parameters are at the top of the file (`Cfg`), with the final values in `variant_S()`:
photo size, bubble size/count, panel thickness, stand angle (`target_tilt`), clearances.
